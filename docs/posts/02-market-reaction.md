Finding 2: the "tone surprise" rarely explains how markets move on the day.

I built a tone surprise (tone minus what the bank's own past statements would predict) and regressed the two-day FX and 2-year-yield moves on it, with a placebo using non-announcement days.

Result: 1 of 11 bank-market pairs passed. The RBA 2-year yield rose about 9 bp per unit of hawkish surprise (R² 0.10).

Everywhere else, nothing that beats its placebo. Pooled across banks, the FX effect is essentially zero. And the LLM did not beat a simple word-list dictionary in any pair after multiple-testing correction.

One cell had a significant coefficient with the opposite sign to the hypothesis. I report it descriptively; the design can't say why.
